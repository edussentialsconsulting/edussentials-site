An introduction to law

Examine what the discipline studies before taking responsibility for a client’s brief.

What is law?

Law examines the rules and institutions through which societies organise authority, recognise rights and obligations, and resolve disputes. Studying it means asking what a rule requires, how it should be interpreted, which facts matter and what follows when those facts are established.

Legal work extends well beyond courtroom advocacy. Lawyers research authorities, interview clients, assess evidence, negotiate terms, draft documents and explain decisions. A word, an exception or a missing condition can change the practical effect of a document.

Law and public policy

Legal analysis asks what the applicable law permits or requires and how it should be interpreted. Policy analysis also considers what rules should achieve, whose interests they affect and whether a different approach would work better. The fields overlap, but these are distinct questions.

The work you will sample

This Lab focuses on commercial contract review. You will compare a complete agreement with an evidence pack, consult two statutory provisions, draft corrections and explain your recommendation to a client.

How law is studied

Expect sustained reading and careful writing. A statute sets out enacted rules; a judgment shows how a court reasons through a dispute. Academic commentary can explain or challenge those materials. You learn to distinguish a source’s authority from the persuasiveness of its argument.

For a concrete example, NLSIU’s Legal Methods course introduces legal sources, academic reading, research and reasoned disagreement. Its law curriculum also illustrates the breadth of study, from contracts and constitutional law to evidence and clinical drafting. Programmes vary.

Your role and reading method

You are assisting the lawyer advising Second Chapter, a fictional book business in Mumbai. Both businesses act through authorised adult representatives. All dates fall within the same fictional December.

Read A, then B, then C, then D. For this controlled exercise, B establishes the agreed instructions: it accepts the specified parts of A and replaces only A3’s payment timing. C is not accepted. D is the draft to check, not evidence that its own terms were approved. This is a source-comparison rule for the case, not a general lesson on contract formation.

Do not invent tax, privacy, ownership or enforceability issues. A revision round means a consolidated set of changes, not one individual edit. Later instructions in Step 6 apply only when you reach that update.

D · Draft agreement for review, 4 December

1. Purpose

PixelWorks will design and deliver a website for Second Chapter’s second-hand book business. The website will present the business, display its books, and publicise its reading events.

2. Website features

The website will contain Home, Book Catalogue, and Events pages. The catalogue will display up to 50 books and include a WhatsApp link for orders. An online payment facility is also included in the agreed project price. Customer accounts are excluded.

3. Materials supplied by the client

Second Chapter will supply the text and photographs needed for the website by 5 December. It will send one consolidated list of feedback by 17 December.

4. Delivery timetable

PixelWorks will provide a working website for review by 15 December. The website will go live no later than 31 December.

If either business expects a delay in completing its responsibilities, it must inform the other promptly. Any change to the agreed timetable must be confirmed by both businesses in writing.

5. Fees and payment

The total project fee is ₹24,000. Second Chapter will pay the full fee when this agreement is signed. PixelWorks will acknowledge receipt of payment in writing.

6. Changes during development

The project price includes any number of rounds of changes to text, photographs, colours, or layout before launch.

Additional pages or functions beyond those listed in Clause 2 require a separate written agreement confirming their price and effect on the timetable before work begins.

7. Access after completion

After launch, PixelWorks will retain sole administrator access to the website. Second Chapter must send future book-listing updates to PixelWorks rather than making those updates itself.

8. Support

PixelWorks will provide reasonable support for a suitable period after launch. The businesses will decide what this includes if a problem arises.

9. Communication and approval

Each business will nominate one contact person. Approvals, feedback, and proposed changes will be recorded by email. Both businesses will keep a copy of the signed agreement and any changes subsequently agreed in writing.

Read the limited statutory passages

Open the Indian Contract Act on India Code. Read Section 37’s opening paragraph, then Section 73’s opening compensation rule and its Explanation. If the PDF fails, the Delhi government reproduction contains these passages. That reproduction also contains historical provisions elsewhere; only the specified passages are used here. The supplied paraphrases below let you continue independently.

Section 37: parties must perform or offer to perform their respective promises, unless performance is excused or dispensed with under the Act or another law.

Section 73: compensation following breach concerns loss arising naturally or known to be likely when contracting; remote and indirect loss is excluded. Available ways of remedying the inconvenience must be considered when estimating loss.

These are educational paraphrases. Assume no legal exception to performance for the limited performance question. This case establishes no actual breach or amount of recoverable loss.

The colleague’s memo

Claim 1: “Because PixelWorks is being paid, it is the only business with promises to perform.”

Claim 2: “If launch is late, every lost sale the client names must automatically be compensated. We do not need to ask what happened or what alternatives were available.”

Use two short paragraphs, one per claim. Identify the section and source used; state its relevant rule and qualification; apply a specific case fact or explain a missing fact; conclude whether the claim is supported. For Claim 2, name two items of evidence you would need before advising on a loss claim. Do not invent that evidence.

Revise your advice when the brief changes

A feasible price is not enough if the deadline fails; a timely delivery is not enough if authority to spend is exceeded.

E · New instructions arrive on 6 December

The client now asks whether online payments can be included. PixelWorks confirms that the original scope can still launch by 20 December for ₹24,000. It offers these alternatives:

  • E1 · Offer A: add online payments for ₹4,000 extra; keep launch on 20 December.
  • E2 · Offer B: add online payments for ₹2,000 extra; launch on 24 December.
  • E3 · Client authority: extra spending must not exceed ₹3,000 and launch must remain no later than 20 December. Both limits are firm. No further supplier option is available in this exercise.
  • E4 · Status: neither addition has been accepted. Client accounts remain excluded. Any addition needs separate written agreement on price and timing.

Decision rule: recommend an addition only if it meets both limits. Otherwise recommend the original scope and defer the addition unless the client changes its instructions. Do not invent a discount or assume the client will accept a delay.

Evaluate the options

Your client advice

Write a subject line and a concise email. Explain the six corrections before signing, highlight three practical priorities, then compare both new offers with both limits and recommend the next step. Explain why an unaccepted proposal must not be inserted as an agreed obligation. Distinguish correcting the draft from approving extra work.

Review the reasoning

  • Each issue is tied to accepted evidence, with a concrete consequence.
  • Each correction preserves all correct details and adds no unauthorised term.
  • The research note separates the source’s rule from facts the file does not establish.
  • The final advice addresses the changed request and both limits.
