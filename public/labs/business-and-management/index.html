Where is everyone waiting?

The manager can see the line at the payment counter from outside the stall. From inside, you can also see the people holding paid tickets. Before you move anyone, follow one order through the whole stall.

From payment to collection

Customers choose a wrap or a bowl when they pay. The cashier sends the ticket to packing, where volunteers either pack the meal or take a matching one from the shelf. At collection, a volunteer checks the ticket and hands over lunch. Everyone stays in arrival order, including customers whose meals are ready in advance.

Operations management looks at how people and materials move through a process. Here the manager could report a record number of payments while customers are still waiting for lunch. In a management course, you might map that process, time each station and change one part at a time to see whether customers actually benefit.

Your brief from the manager

Design a shift that hands over at least 23 meals by the end of minute four and leaves at most two advance-packed meals unused once everyone has eaten. Meet both targets on two different breaks. Your final note should tell the next shift where to put its spare volunteer, how many meals to have ready and what to watch once orders start coming in.

What one minute means

The original team can take six payments, pack four fresh meals and hand over eight lunches per minute. One spare volunteer can staff a second payment counter (+6 payments), join packing (+4 fresh meals) or join collection (+8 handovers). The other stations keep their original teams.

At the start of minutes one to four, 10, 10, 8 and 4 customers arrive respectively. No one else joins. In each minute customers pay first, then packing, then collection. A ticket can move through all three in that minute. A customer arriving at the start of minute two and collecting at the end of minute three has waited two minutes.

What should be ready when the shutters open?

The kitchen offers a shelf beside packing. A meal prepared before the break takes no fresh packing slot when a matching ticket comes up, but a bowl cannot fill an order for a wrap.

The kitchen’s order book

Yesterday’s early break sold 20 wraps and 12 bowls. The later break sold 12 wraps and 20 bowls. In the first replay today, customers ordered 26 wraps and six bowls. You do not yet have the second rush’s orders.

You can prepare up to eight meals in any mix before opening. During service, packing uses a matching prepared meal first and otherwise packs to order. Prepared meals count towards the service target only at handover. An unused meal still occupies its shelf space when service ends.
